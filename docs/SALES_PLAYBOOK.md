# PlaceAI Sales Playbook

## Ideal first customers

Prioritize institutions where placement operations still depend on spreadsheets, WhatsApp groups, email chains, shared drives and manually collected resumes.

Best initial segments:

- private engineering colleges with 500–5,000 placement-active students;
- MBA / management institutes with centralized placement cells;
- training institutes and coding bootcamps with employer placement programs;
- emerging universities that need better recruiter governance and reporting;
- placement consultancies running institution-specific hiring programs.

The primary buyer is normally the Placement Director, Training & Placement Officer (TPO), Career Services Head, Dean/Director responsible for employability, or a management stakeholder overseeing placement outcomes.

## Core sales proposition

> Replace fragmented placement operations with one institution-controlled workflow for student readiness, recruiter access, campus drives, applications and outcomes.

Do not position PlaceAI initially as another job board. Lead with operational control, privacy, reporting and student readiness.

## Buyer pain points to qualify

Ask how the institution currently handles:

1. student master data and verification;
2. recruiter onboarding and job approvals;
3. CGPA / branch / batch eligibility rules;
4. resume sharing and candidate access;
5. application status tracking;
6. offer and placement outcome reporting;
7. interview preparation and employability readiness;
8. management reports and accreditation evidence.

A strong prospect usually has at least three of these workflows spread across disconnected tools.

## Suggested pilot offer

Start with one graduating batch and a limited recruiter group for 30–60 days.

Suggested pilot success metrics:

- placement-office administrative hours saved;
- percentage of student profiles completed;
- time from recruiter job submission to campus approval;
- application turnaround time;
- recruiter response / completion rate;
- time required to produce placement outcome reports;
- student engagement with readiness and interview tools.

## Pricing hypothesis for early sales

Use this only as an initial commercial framework; validate with prospects before fixing public prices.

- Pilot: ₹25,000–₹75,000 for one batch / short implementation window.
- Small institution annual plan: ₹1.5–₹3 lakh/year.
- Mid-size institution: ₹3–₹8 lakh/year depending on students, recruiters, support and integrations.
- Enterprise/university group: custom annual contract with SSO, integrations, SLA, migration and security requirements.

Avoid selling on AI tokens. Sell operational outcomes, governance and reporting.

## Outreach sequence

Day 1: concise email/LinkedIn message identifying the spreadsheet/manual-workflow problem and asking for a 15-minute walkthrough.

Day 3–4: send one specific workflow example, such as campus job approval + eligibility + recruiter pipeline in one system.

Day 7: send a short pilot proposal with one graduating batch and measurable success criteria.

Day 12–14: final follow-up focused on whether placement process modernization is a priority this academic cycle.

## Outreach message

Subject: Placement workflow pilot for <Institution>

Hello <Name>,

I am working on PlaceAI, an institution-controlled placement operating system for colleges and training institutes. It combines verified student records, recruiter access, campus job approvals, eligibility rules, application tracking, placement outcomes and AI-assisted student readiness in one workflow.

I am looking for a small number of institutions to run a controlled pilot with one graduating batch. The goal is to reduce spreadsheet/manual coordination and make recruiter and outcome tracking easier for the placement office.

Would a 15-minute product walkthrough be useful this week?

Regards,
<Your Name>

## Walkthrough close

Do not end a walkthrough with “what do you think?”. Ask for a concrete next step:

> If we configure one graduating batch and two recruiters, who needs to approve a 30-day pilot and what data/security questions must we answer first?
